Hi,

I'm Lukas, and I'll be your guide for the next ten weeks. Thanks for signing up for *Generative AI in Research: Use It Well, Use It Critically*.

A quick note on what this course is and isn't. It's not a tutorial for a specific tool - those go stale too fast. It's not a cheerleading exercise about how AI will transform research, nor a lament about AI slop destroying it. We need to collectively shape the direction, and that starts with understanding the tools. That said, understanding the tools isn't the only question worth asking. There are real ethical concerns about *whether* to use them at all - the environmental costs, the exploitative labour behind training data, the concentration of power in a handful of companies. This course doesn't try to settle those. Instead, it focuses on *how* to use AI responsibly if you choose to: transparency, integrity, knowing where to keep human judgment. Those questions run through the whole course, not as a separate module but as part of how we approach every topic. The course is not aimed only at beginners. Whether you've barely touched AI or you're already using coding agents, the goal is the same: to build habits for working with AI that are effective, critical, and sustainable.

**How the course works**

You'll get two emails a week for ten weeks.

- **Monday ("Spark")**: An introduction to the core concept and a hands-on exercise. These take about 10-15 minutes if you do the exercise, which you should aim for. Reading about AI is not the same as using it.
- **Thursday ("Anchor")**: A shorter email that deepens or complicates what Monday introduced - often also with a small exercise and a journal prompt.

The emails are designed to be read in order, but life happens. If you fall behind, you can catch up at your own pace. Nothing expires.

**What we'll cover**

The course follows a progression:

- **Weeks 1-3**: How LLMs work, where they fail, and how to prompt them well
- **Week 4**: Working with your own documents - grounded and retrieval-augmented tools
- **Week 5**: Writing with AI - when polish helps and when it flattens
- **Week 6**: AI for coding and data analysis
- **Weeks 7-8**: AI agents and structured workflows
- **Weeks 9-10**: Disclosure, collaboration, and clarifying your own AI practice

Each week builds on the last, but we'll keep circling back to the core question: where does AI actually help your work, and where do you need to stay in the driver's seat?

**Two things to set up**

1. **A journal.** Nothing fancy - a Google Doc, a text file, a notebook. Most emails end with a prompt to reflect on what you've tried. The journal is for you, but the final weeks will be more useful when you can look back at how your thinking shifted.
